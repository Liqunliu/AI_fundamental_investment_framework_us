# Judgment Examples — US Company Qualitative Ratings

> Calibration examples for moat, management, and MD&A ratings.
> These examples illustrate the expected depth and consistency of qualitative judgments.
> Use as reference points, not rigid rules — every company requires independent analysis.

## Moat Rating Examples

### WIDE Moat — MSFT (Light-asset platform)

```
Layer 1:
  Switching costs: STRONG — Enterprise customers deeply integrated (Active Directory, Office 365,
    Azure). Migration cost estimated at 2-3 years + $10M+ for large enterprises.
  Network effects: STRONG — Developer ecosystem (VS Code, GitHub, npm). More developers →
    more tools → more developers.
  Intangible assets: MODERATE — Brand trust in enterprise, but consumer brand less differentiated.

Layer 2:
  Data asset barriers: STRONG — Azure telemetry, GitHub Copilot training data (35B+ lines of code)
  Algorithm barriers: MODERATE — OpenAI partnership provides frontier models, but not fully proprietary
  AI investment: STRONG — Copilot products, Azure AI services, first-mover in enterprise AI

Cross-layer: YES — Enterprise switching costs → more usage data → better AI products → higher
  switching costs. Compound flywheel active.

Moat: WIDE (Business: Switching costs + Network effects | Technical: Data flywheel + AI)
Compound flywheel: YES
```

### NARROW Moat — HD (Capital-hungry retail)

```
Layer 1:
  Scale economies: MODERATE — 2,300+ stores provide distribution density, but not unassailable
  Cost advantage: MODERATE — Volume purchasing power over smaller competitors
  Switching costs: WEAK — Customers can easily shop at Lowe's, local hardware stores
  Brand: MODERATE — Recognized brand, but not commanding significant price premium

Layer 2:
  Not applicable — Traditional retail, no meaningful technical barriers

Moat: NARROW (Business: Scale economies + Cost advantage | Technical: not applicable)
Compound flywheel: NO
```

### NONE Moat — Generic retailer / commodity producer

```
Layer 1:
  All barriers: WEAK or ABSENT
  No meaningful scale advantage, easy switching, commodity products, no brand premium

Layer 2:
  Not applicable

Moat: NONE
```

## Management Rating Examples

### Excellent — Tim Cook at AAPL

```
Tenure: CEO since 2011 (13+ years)
Capital allocation:
  - Returned $700B+ to shareholders via buybacks and dividends since 2012
  - Share count reduced from 6.6B to 15.4B (split-adjusted) — consistent buyback discipline
  - Services revenue grew from $16B to $85B+ (new high-margin revenue stream)
  - Minimal goodwill impairments, no failed major acquisitions
  - Maintained net cash position while returning massive capital
Rating: Excellent — consistent, disciplined capital allocation with strong returns
```

### Adequate — typical stable-company CEO

```
Tenure: 5+ years
Capital allocation:
  - Steady dividend growth (~5% annually), regular buybacks
  - 1-2 medium-sized acquisitions, integrated without major impairments
  - No significant value destruction, but no exceptional capital allocation either
  - Related-party transactions: None or minimal
Rating: Adequate — competent stewardship without exceptional alpha generation
```

### Destroying value — hypothetical example

```
Tenure: 7 years
Capital allocation:
  - $5B+ in acquisitions at premium valuations → subsequent $2B goodwill impairment
  - Dividend cut despite sufficient cash flow (to fund acquisitions)
  - Share dilution from equity-funded deals
  - CEO compensation increased 40% while TSR underperformed sector
  - Related-party transactions: CEO's family member contracted as consultant
Rating: Destroying value → VETO
```

### Observation period — new CEO post turnaround

```
Prior CEO: Destroying value (excessive acquisitions, $3B impairment)
New CEO: Appointed 18 months ago
Observable signals:
  - POSITIVE: Wrote down remaining $500M goodwill in first quarter (kitchen-sink quarter)
  - POSITIVE: Announced $2B accelerated buyback program
  - NEUTRAL: Exploring strategic alternatives for non-core division
  - NOT YET ASSESSED: No full fiscal year completed under new management
Rating: Observation — predecessor damage being cleared, early signals positive
  Note: Factor 3 should apply extra discount to predictability (new management = uncertainty)
```

## MD&A Credibility Examples

### HIGH Credibility

```
Historical track record:
  FY2022 guidance: "Revenue growth 8-10%" → Actual: 9.2% ✓
  FY2023 guidance: "Margin expansion 50-100bps" → Actual: +70bps ✓
  FY2024 guidance: "Revenue growth 6-8%, margin stable" → Actual: 7.1%, margin +20bps ✓

Characteristics:
  - Quantified guidance with narrow ranges
  - Consistent delivery within guided ranges
  - Transparent discussion of headwinds alongside tailwinds
  - Specific risk mitigation measures (not boilerplate)

Rating: HIGH — management guidance is a reliable forward indicator
```

### MEDIUM Credibility

```
Historical track record:
  FY2022 guidance: "Mid-single-digit revenue growth" → Actual: 3.1% (below implied range)
  FY2023 guidance: "Improving margins" → Actual: margins flat (directional miss)
  FY2024 guidance: "Return to growth" → Actual: 5.2% revenue growth ✓

Characteristics:
  - Mostly directional guidance (not quantified)
  - Mixed delivery track record
  - Risk discussion present but somewhat generic

Rating: MEDIUM — directionally useful but not reliable for precise estimates
```

### LOW Credibility

```
Historical track record:
  FY2022 guidance: "Expect strong growth" → Actual: revenue declined 8%
  FY2023 guidance: "Accelerating momentum" → Actual: further 3% decline
  FY2024 guidance: "Inflection point reached" → Actual: flat growth

Characteristics:
  - Vague, promotional language
  - Consistent overestimation of prospects
  - Risk factors are boilerplate / unchanged year over year
  - No accountability for prior guidance misses

Rating: LOW — management narrative is unreliable, discount forward statements heavily
```

## SBC Concern Level Examples

### LOW — V (Visa)

```
SBC/Revenue: 2.5%
SBC/Net Income: 5.8%
Net share dilution (5yr): -3.2% (shares decreased — buybacks exceed SBC dilution)
Assessment: SBC is minimal relative to earnings, fully offset by buybacks
```

### MODERATE — GOOGL (Alphabet)

```
SBC/Revenue: 6.8%
SBC/Net Income: 12.4%
Net share dilution (5yr): -1.5% (buybacks mostly offset SBC)
Assessment: Meaningful but manageable; buybacks offset most dilution
```

### HIGH — SNOW (Snowflake, hypothetical)

```
SBC/Revenue: 35%
SBC/Net Income: >100% (company would be unprofitable excluding SBC)
Net share dilution (5yr): +8.2% (significant net dilution)
Assessment: SBC is the primary form of compensation; GAAP earnings are meaningless
  without SBC adjustment. Must use adjusted metrics throughout analysis.
```
